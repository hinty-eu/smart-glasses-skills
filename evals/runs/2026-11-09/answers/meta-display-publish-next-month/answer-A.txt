I can't verify the current state of Meta's developer access from here, and it's the deciding factor, so treat the specifics below as things to confirm.

**What I believe is true (check each against Meta's current docs)**
- Meta Ray-Ban Display is a separate product from the camera/audio Ray-Ban Meta glasses. It has a small monocular display and is controlled with the Neural Band.
- It pairs to a phone through Meta's companion app (Meta AI), on both iOS and Android.
- Meta's Wearables Device Access Toolkit launched as a developer preview aimed at camera and audio. When I last had information, display access for third-party apps was described as "coming later", and publishing to the general public was limited.
- Availability was initially US-only and through limited retail channels.

**Possible build paths, in order of preference**
1. **Official display API or toolkit, if it's now open to you.**
   - Your backend pushes cards to your phone app (Android and iOS). The app uses Meta's SDK to render them on the glasses.
   - You'd need to build both mobile integrations, plus pairing, auth and a fallback when the glasses are disconnected.
   - The card format would be constrained by the display's layout, size and input model.
2. **Meta's web or "app" surface for the display, if one exists.** If Meta supports a hosted or web-based card experience, your backend could serve it directly, with minimal mobile work.
3. **Phone notification mirroring.** If the glasses mirror phone notifications, you send high-quality push notifications and they appear on the glasses. It's the cheapest option, but you get no custom layout, no checklist interaction, and little control over timing.

**Is one month realistic?**
Probably not for "all users on both platforms", for two reasons that have nothing to do with your engineering:
- **Access.** If you're not already in Meta's developer program with display access, you may be blocked on their approval and publishing rules. Those are outside your control, and a month is short for that.
- **Reach.** The device has been limited in market and volume. Even a perfect launch would reach only the small share of your users who own one. "All users" probably means "all users who own the glasses, in supported countries".
